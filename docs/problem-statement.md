# Problem Statement

## Background

Port terminal operations are among the most complex real-time logistics environments in the
world. A major container port handles hundreds of vessel calls per month, thousands of
containers per day, and coordinates dozens of moving resources — berths, ship-to-shore
cranes, rubber-tyred gantry cranes, yard trucks, and gate lanes — all simultaneously.

The global supply chain depends on ports operating efficiently. A single hour of delay at
a major hub port can cascade into days of delays across shipping lines, warehouses, and
retail distribution networks.

## The Problem

Port operations managers at large container terminals currently lack a unified
AI-assisted decision-support system. Their challenges include:

1. **Reactive, not predictive operations.** Congestion is typically identified only after
   it has already developed — not hours before arrival. Operators have no reliable 24–72
   hour congestion forecast that explains *why* risk is rising.

2. **Fragmented tooling.** Vessel ETA data sits in one system, crane assignments in
   another, yard utilisation in a third, and disruption tracking in spreadsheets. There
   is no single pane of glass.

3. **No AI-assisted prioritisation.** When 8 vessels are approaching simultaneously and
   only 4 berths are available, the decision of which vessel gets which berth, which
   crane, and which yard zone is made by gut feel — not by data-driven AI analysis.

4. **No disruption simulation.** When a crane fails or a labour strike begins, there is
   no tool to rapidly model the downstream impact and generate a structured response plan.

5. **No natural-language operational interface.** Operations managers cannot ask
   plain-language questions like *"Which vessel should I prioritise?"* and receive a
   structured, data-backed answer in seconds.

## Who is Affected

- **Port Operations Managers** at large container terminals (100,000+ TEU/month)
- **Terminal Operators** responsible for berth planning and crane assignment
- **Logistics Coordinators** at shipping lines monitoring vessel status
- **Supply Chain Managers** dependent on reliable container delivery windows

## Why It Matters

Port congestion has measurable, quantifiable costs:

- **Vessel demurrage** (the fee charged when a vessel waits beyond its contracted time)
  averages $15,000–$50,000 per day for large container ships.
- **Yard dwell time** increases when containers cannot be collected on schedule,
  compounding congestion in a feedback loop.
- **Reefer (refrigerated) cargo** spoils if not processed within tight time windows.
- **Supply chain visibility** degrades for every hour a vessel is unaccounted for.

A one-hour reduction in average vessel waiting time across a terminal handling 300 vessel
calls per month could save millions of dollars annually in demurrage alone.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Current tools used by port operators:

- **Port Community Systems (PCS):** Focus on document exchange and regulatory compliance,
  not operational AI decision support.
- **Terminal Operating Systems (TOS):** Manage execution (crane moves, truck lanes) but
  do not provide predictive analytics or AI-assisted recommendations.
- **Manual spreadsheets and whiteboards:** Still common for berth planning in many ports.
  Error-prone, not real-time, impossible to share across teams instantly.
- **Generic BI dashboards:** Show historical data but do not answer *"What should I do
  right now?"* or simulate future disruption scenarios.

**PortMind AI addresses the gap between data and decision** — the layer between raw
operational data and the specific, actionable recommendation the operations manager
needs to act on immediately.
